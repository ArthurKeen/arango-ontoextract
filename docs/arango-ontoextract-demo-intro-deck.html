Arango-OntoExtract

Living ontologies from documents and databases

Turning the model of your business that nobody wrote down into something machines can query, validate and reason over.

OntoExtract — talk + demo → CDF

~30s. "Every company has a model of its own business that exists nowhere a machine can read. It's in the decks, and it's implied by the database schema. This is a system that goes and gets it."

Set the shape of the session: about five minutes of framing, then straight into the running system. Then CDF.

The problem

The knowledge is already yours. Nothing can use it.

Documents

PDFs, decks, specs. They describe how the business thinks — but it's prose. You can search it; you can't reason over it.

Database schemas

Tables and keys already encode a working model — but only physically. Nothing says "a Customer is a kind of Party."

An ontology is the bridge

A machine-readable model of what exists and how it relates: classes, the properties between them, the hierarchy, and the constraints.

Think of it as a schema that carries meaning instead of storage layout.

The catch: authoring one by hand is slow, expensive, needs rare expertise — and it rots the moment the sources change.

~60s. Don't teach OWL. The line that lands: "a database schema that carries meaning instead of just storage layout."

Ask the room: could you produce one diagram, machine-readable, saying what your core business entities are and how they relate? Almost nobody can. That's the gap.

What AOE does

The LLM proposes. The human disposes.

Not one prompt — a pipeline of specialised agents, with a mandatory stop before anything is committed.

extract→ check consistency→ judge quality→ resolve duplicates
→ reconcile beliefs→ repair structure→ test every is-a→ human curation

The one rule everything follows

Every class and relationship carries the evidence that justifies it — the exact chunk, slide or table. Nothing reaches the curated ontology without a person, or an explicit written policy, saying yes.

~60s. Don't walk all eight nodes. The point is the shape: specialised steps, each auditable, ending at a human gate.

"Propose / dispose" is the phrase to repeat — everything in the demo follows from taking it literally.

Why it's trustworthy enough to keep

Four guarantees you'll see in the demo

GroundedClick any concept and trace it to the slide or table it came from.
ReversibleNothing is overwritten. Every class is versioned in time — scrub the timeline and watch the graph redraw as it stood.
Revised, not redoneA second document updates what the ontology already believes. Only genuine contradictions reach a human.
Durable curationA curator's decision survives the next re-extraction — structurally, not by convention.

~75s. This is the slide that earns the demo. Each row maps to something they will literally watch happen.

The differentiator question to leave with them: "ask any tool in this category what happens to a human's correction the next time the machine runs." Most cannot answer it.

Getting started is not the hard part

Three ways in — two need no LLM at all

A database you runTables become classes, foreign keys relationships, columns attributes. A deterministic walk of a live schema — nothing to hallucinate. Relational and ArangoDB.
Published standardsEleven vocabularies — BFO, SKOS, PROV-O, Schema.org, SOSA/SSN, FIBO — one click, bundled offline.
Your documentsPDFs, decks, specs — including the taxonomies that only exist inside diagrams.

Most people's fastest first result is a schema they already own — a defensible ontology in about a minute.

~60s. Useful for the commercial question "how long before we see anything?" Answer: minutes, and the first path needs no API key.

Over to the system

What to watch for

1A document becomes a proposed ontology — and every concept shows its evidence.
2The curator's gate: approve, reject, rename, reparent — on one canvas, no wizard.
3The timeline — scrub back and see the ontology as it was.
4A second source: beliefs update instead of starting over.
5Everything is queryable, exportable, and drivable by an AI agent over MCP.
